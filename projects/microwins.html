01 / The context

Make the next step feel achievable.

Large goals and long task lists can feel overwhelming. Our mobile development team built MicroWins around a smaller unit of progress: a daily achievement that a person can complete, acknowledge, and build on.

The app brings daily wins, mood check-ins, streaks, and weekly reflection into one focused iOS experience.

02 / Team contribution

A complete everyday flow.

I collaborated with a four-person team on interface design, SwiftUI implementation, navigation and logic, testing, and debugging. The resulting app supports:

  • Adding, completing, reopening, and deleting daily wins.
  • Recording a mood for the day.
  • Viewing daily completion progress and a current streak.
  • Reviewing completed wins and progress across the week.

The project is shared team work. The repository documents the contributors, application structure, and setup instructions.

03 / Engineering choices

Keep state understandable.

The app uses MVVM to separate stored data and app logic from the SwiftUI views. Models represent wins and mood entries, while MicroWinsStore manages state and persistence.

NavigationStack and TabView connect the dashboard, list, mood, and summary screens. Local JSON files let the app load saved entries on launch and preserve changes without requiring a server or account.

That scope keeps the focus on the core experience: a small action should lead to a visible, saved change across the relevant screens.

04 / What I took forward

Simple experiences still need careful engineering.

A completion checkmark can affect today's count, a streak, and a weekly summary. This project strengthened my attention to shared state, reusable views, persistence, and consistent navigation.

It also gave me experience building within a team: aligning interfaces and data models, integrating changes, and testing the flow that connects everyone's work.
